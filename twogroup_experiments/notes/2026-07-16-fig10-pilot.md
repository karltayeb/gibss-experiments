# fig10 pilot - causal PIP vs threshold (SER simulation)

Goal: replace the earlier "detection strength" proxy with a real SER simulation
reporting **average causal PIP** across ~200 datasets, for the actual methods, and
find a realistic regime showing the expected orderings. Priority per review.

## Setup (real fits, pre-migration pipeline)

- Datasets: `core.simulate(spec, replicate)`; spec from
  `loader.resolve_simulation(design, enrichment, signal, error)`.
- Fits: `loader.run_method(coord, sim)`; causal PIP = `single_effects[0]["alpha"][causal]`.
- Design `gaussian_rho0.50_n500_p100`, enrichment `ser_b2` (effect 2, intercept -2),
  gaussian error. Signal driven by **scale** (`scale_s` -> f1 = N(0, s^2)), the
  clean single-axis way to make f1 poorly identified.
- Methods: `twogroup_oracle`, `twogroup` (estimated loc+scale), `cox_reversed`,
  `cox__threshold={1,2,3,4}` (censored), `logistic_threshold={1,2,3,4}`.
- Scripts: `slides/scripts/fig10_experiment.py` (200 reps -> `figures/fig10_data.json`,
  provenance-stamped) and `slides/scripts/fig10_pip_vs_threshold.py` (plots the cache).

## Scale sweep (mean causal PIP +/- SE, 120 reps)

| signal | oracle | estimated | cox_rev | cox@1 | cox@2 | cox@3 | cox_rev - est |
|--------|--------|-----------|---------|-------|-------|-------|---------------|
| scale_0.75 | 0.040 | 0.026 | 0.089 | 0.046 | 0.070 | 0.027 | **+0.063** |
| scale_1.0  | 0.183 | 0.139 | 0.212 | 0.108 | 0.159 | 0.074 | **+0.073** |
| scale_1.5  | 0.789 | 0.751 | 0.747 | 0.395 | 0.630 | 0.351 | -0.004 |
| scale_1.75 | 0.946 | 0.942 | 0.909 | 0.621 | 0.842 | 0.622 | -0.033 |

(SE ~0.02-0.03 at 120 reps.)

## Findings

1. **Both hard requirements hold at scale_1.5**: oracle (0.80) > estimated (0.77)
   > cox-reversed (0.76), all threshold-free; cox and logistic peak at tau=2 then
   collapse by tau=4 (clear threshold sensitivity). This is the chosen setting for
   the figure. Caveat: the top-three gaps are small (~1-2 SE at 200 reps) - the big,
   robust separation is threshold-free cluster vs the binarizing methods.

2. **cox-reversed > estimated appears only at low SNR** (scale <= 1.0): at scale_1.0,
   cox_rev 0.212 > est 0.139 (+0.073, ~3 SE) - the rank method beats the
   poorly-identified two-group, confirming the intuition directionally. **But** there
   oracle (0.183) also falls below cox_rev, so "oracle on top" breaks. The full chain
   `oracle > cox-reversed > estimated` does **not** co-occur in this design: raising SNR
   to keep oracle on top also rescues the estimated two-group.

3. Interpretation: in this design the two-group estimator is robust - it's only
   beaten by ranks when the whole problem is failing. Getting a clean
   oracle > cox-reversed > estimated likely needs a regime where f1 estimation is
   hampered *independent* of SNR.

## Open / next (to get the clean triple ordering, if wanted)

- More reps (500-1000) to resolve the top-three ordering at scale_1.5.
- Harder design (larger p, higher correlation) to amplify localization gaps.
- Deliberate misspecification: fit the **location-family** two-group (`twogroup_loc_fam`)
  to **scale**-driven data - a genuinely poorly-identified f1 that ranks should beat
  while the oracle stays correct.
- Productionize as a supercollection (add to `004_scale_snr` or a new experiment)

## Follow-up: misspecification pilot (slide 8)

Review concluded the "barely identifiable" framing was really finite-sample weak
identification; the practical issue is **f1 family misspecification**. Pilot
(`slides/scripts/fig08_experiment.py`, 150 sims, scale_1.5, mean causal PIP):

| method | mean PIP |
|--------|----------|
| twogroup_oracle | 0.79 |
| twogroup_scale_fam (correct family) | 0.76 |
| twogroup_loc_fam (misspecified) | **0.04** |
| cox_reversed (models no f1) | 0.75 |

Misspecifying the f1 family (location-family on scale-driven data) **collapses**
the two-group; the rank method, which never models f1, is unaffected and matches
the correctly-specified / oracle two-group. This became slide 8
(`fig08_misspecification.py`), replacing the identifiability-ridge figure (kept as
backup). It ties slide 8 to the talk's thesis: robustness of rank methods to what
you can't model.
